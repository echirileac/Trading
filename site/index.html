              How the forecast works

              • Prices: NATGAS uses the NYMEX Henry Hub front-month futures quote from CNBC plus its own daily history; every other market comes from Twelve Data (daily history once a day, live quotes every 10–15 minutes to stay within the free plan).
              • News: stories from a whitelist of trusted outlets (Reuters, Bloomberg, NGI, EIA, Barchart, FXEmpire, S&P Global, Energy Intelligence, Rigzone and others) found with a search built for each market, refreshed about every 15 minutes. The server opens each article and reads its full text where the publisher allows it; every relevant sentence is scored with a keyword list for that kind of market (natural gas: weather, storage, production, LNG; oil: OPEC, inventories; metals: dollar, rates, safe-haven demand; stocks and indices: earnings, guidance, rates; crypto: ETF flows, regulation; plus general market language). The article body counts for 70% of a story's score and the headline 30%; paywalled stories fall back to the headline. Stories are weighted by recency.
              • Stocks and ETFs: because keyword lists catch mostly generic words for a company, each new headline is also read once by a free AI model (gpt-oss on Groq). It says whether the story is really about the company and rates its likely effect on the share price over 1 to 5 sessions from -2 to +2, with a short reason shown under the story. That rating counts for 75% of the story's score and the keyword score of the article text for 25%; stories judged not to be about the company are left out. If the AI is unavailable, keyword scoring is used.
              • Charts: the Live chart is TradingView's free embedded chart with its own data feed (real-time for forex and crypto, delayed for stocks; CFD prices for commodities and indices). The Forecast view draws the model's lines on the daily prices the forecast itself uses.
              • Timeframes: on every refresh the 15-minute, 1-hour and daily charts are read with the same fixed textbook rules. Trend: price against its 20- and 50-bar averages and whether highs and lows are rising or falling (ADX says how strong the trend is). Momentum: RSI and MACD. Patterns: engulfing bars, hammers and shooting stars, morning and evening stars, double tops and bottoms, head and shoulders, breakouts from the last 20 bars, RSI divergences, pullbacks that end inside a trend, and average crossovers. A pattern counts only once its bar has closed. For natural gas and Brent the 15-minute bars are built from the site's own price samples (about one a minute, so very short spikes can be missed); for Twelve Data markets the exchange's 15-minute candles are fetched about every 15 minutes. Every pattern is scored on this market's own history (was the close 1 hour, 4 hours or 3 sessions later on its side?) and again from the day it went live.
              • Track record: every trading day at the close the site writes down its 1, 3 and 5-day forecasts and later scores each against the actual close. Target dates count trading sessions only: weekends and exchange holidays are skipped (NYSE holidays for stocks and natural gas, ICE closures for Brent; none for currencies, spot metals and crypto). The log is kept on the site (data/h/) and nothing in it is edited afterwards. The backtest replays only the price signals on past closes, so it says how well the ranges are sized, not how good the news reading is.
              • Events: weekly EIA reports follow their usual day and time (they move in US holiday weeks), Fed decision days come from the Fed's published calendar, US inflation (CPI) and jobs report days from the Bureau of Labor Statistics schedule (not for natural gas), and a stock's next earnings date is looked up once a day from Nasdaq and can be an estimate. Events are flagged on the forecast and given to the AI analyst; the ranges are not widened for them.
              • The page itself checks for new data every minute.
              • It is a mechanical keyword model, not a human reading. It can be wrong when an article is mixed, quotes opposing views, or is mainly about something else.

              Not financial advice. CFDs are leveraged and can move fast against you. A broker's CFD quote can differ slightly from the market prices shown here, and futures-based CFDs roll to the next contract a few days before expiry.
