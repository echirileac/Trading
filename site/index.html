        How the forecast works

        • Prices: NATGAS uses the NYMEX Henry Hub front-month futures quote from CNBC plus its own daily history; every other market comes from Twelve Data (daily history once a day, live quotes every 10–15 minutes to stay within the free plan).
        • News: stories from a whitelist of trusted outlets (Reuters, Bloomberg, NGI, EIA, Barchart, FXEmpire, S&P Global, Energy Intelligence, Rigzone and others) found with a search built for each market, refreshed about every 15 minutes. The server opens each article and reads its full text where the publisher allows it; every relevant sentence is scored with a keyword list for that kind of market (natural gas: weather, storage, production, LNG; oil: OPEC, inventories; metals: dollar, rates, safe-haven demand; stocks and indices: earnings, guidance, rates; crypto: ETF flows, regulation; plus general market language). The article body counts for 70% of a story's score and the headline 30%; paywalled stories fall back to the headline. Stories are weighted by recency.
        • The page itself checks for new data every minute.
        • It is a mechanical keyword model, not a human reading. It can be wrong when an article is mixed, quotes opposing views, or is mainly about something else.

        Not financial advice. CFDs are leveraged and can move fast against you. A broker's CFD quote can differ slightly from the market prices shown here, and futures-based CFDs roll to the next contract a few days before expiry.
